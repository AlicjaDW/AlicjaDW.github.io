Case study 02

Who owns what: a cross-sell map of the customer base

One view of every customer's product holdings, showing where the next sale is most likely.

Sector
Automotive and energy retail
Audience
Sales and home energy leadership
Tools
Python, JavaScript, Canvas charts

Built with fictional sample data. The layout and logic match the working version.

Explore live dashboard

The result

For the first time, leadership saw sales across every product category in one place. Around 70% of partner buyers turned out to be existing customers.

The business question

Ownership of the core product and each add-on sat in separate extracts. Leadership couldn't size the main opportunity pool (core product only) or the step-up pool (one add-on, not yet the second). They also couldn't tell whether long-standing customers held more products than recent ones.

What I built

  • A roll-up to the individual, not the account, so a corporate buyer doesn't collapse into a single customer.
  • Four exclusive ownership segments, each with what it means commercially.
  • A comparison of single-item and multi-item households, and of first-time and repeat buyers.
  • Attach rates tracked by year of first purchase, so older and newer cohorts can be compared.
  • A split of partner orders between existing and new customers, with what existing customers already owned.

What changed

The finding that around 70% of partner buyers were existing customers shifted targeting toward the base. Likely buyers were then clustered for campaigns.

Why it matters in luxury retail

This is share-of-wallet analysis: which clients have bought one category but not the next, and which cohorts deepen their relationship over time. It's the question behind every clienteling and CRM programme.
